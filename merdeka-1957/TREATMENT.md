# Merdeka, 1957: treatment

Style: Comic History Cartoon · 1080×1920 (9:16) · 24 fps · 10 s · English narrator (Kokoro bm_george), burned-in captions (`subtitles_en.srt`).

> Final timings are in §11, "As built". Where they differ from §4–§8 (written before production), §11 wins.

## Brief
- Topic: the Federation of Malaya's independence from Britain, 31 August 1957.
- Two sides, two puppets: **Britain** (a colonial administrator: white suit, pith helmet; personifies the country, not a real person) and **Malaya** (baju Melayu, songkok, sampin). No real people's likenesses, no real flags.
- Images: the user makes the part sheets with their own image tool from `SHEETS.md`.
- Honesty note: independence was negotiated (the London talks, 1956), not won in battle, so the paper action is a peel that Britain finally lets go of, not a violent tear.

## 1. Three candidate structures
1. **Layer on layer, peeled in one go.** The peninsula carries a stack of patches (Portuguese, Dutch, British). Malaya pulls them all off. *Rejected*: 10 s can't hold three powers, and it suggests 1957 undid Portugal and the Netherlands, which is wrong.
2. **Stamp the papers.** A civilian's document has "BRITISH PROTECTED PERSON" stamped over with "CITIZEN OF MALAYA". *Rejected as the main shape*: it's quiet and needs reading time; the 3-second hook is weak.
3. **It won't lie flat → it lets go.** Britain's patch sits over the peninsula with a curling corner. Malaya hops in, grips the corner and tugs. Britain strains, then shrugs and lets go. The patch peels away and Malaya's own colour shows underneath. "MERDEKA!" **Chosen**: one subject (Malaya), one goal (its own map back), one turn (the release). The native move "it won't lie flat" plays at the peak and is turned on its head: this time it does come off.

Opening (not the demo's hop-in): the scissors and helmet already in frame, with the corner of the patch flapping in the first frame. Ending: a pin drops on Kuala Lumpur with its date slip. Score shape: an oom-pah for Britain, a gendang/rebana pulse for Malaya that takes over after the release.

## 2. Logline and arc
In ten seconds, Malaya peels Britain's paper patch off its own map, and Britain finally lets go.
- Setup (0–3 s): Britain's patch pressed onto the peninsula, one corner curling up.
- Turn (3–7 s): Malaya grips the corner, tug of war, near-silence, Britain lets go, the patch peels away.
- Ending (7–10 s): "MERDEKA!" A pin on Kuala Lumpur, 31 AUG 1957.

## 5. Beat sheet
- 0.0 Patch on, corner flapping; Britain leaning on it. Year tag "1957" pasted top left. VO starts.
- 1.0 Caption 1.
- 3.0 Malaya hops in (xylophone steps), squats, grips the corner.
- 3.8, 4.4, 5.0 Three tugs (on the beat); each lifts the corner further; Britain trembles.
- 5.6 Near-silence (tremolo); Britain's head swaps to a resigned face; shrug.
- 6.2 Peel: the patch rips upward, shadow offset 40 px, flutters off frame top. Malaya's printed colour revealed. Helmet hop + slide whistle.
- 6.8 Bubble: "MERDEKA!" (Malaya, arm up).
- 7.6 Drop to the date panel; the pin lands on Kuala Lumpur (stamp thump, music stops dead).
- 8.0–10.0 Date slip "31 AUG 1957"; hold; pull back to the page.

## 6. Cue map
- 120 BPM, 4/4, 0.5 s a beat; 10 s = 5 bars.
- Bars 1–2 (0–4 s): tuba + trombone oom-pah (Britain).
- Bar 3 (4–6 s): oom-pah thins; rebana hits on each tug (3.8 / 4.4 / 5.0 are off the grid on purpose: struggle); tremolo from 5.6.
- 6.0 downbeat of bar 4: the peel. A gendang + gong pulse takes over, bright glockenspiel on "MERDEKA".
- 7.6: drop; 8.0 downbeat of bar 5: the pin lands, music stops dead, then one soft gong tail to 10 s.

- Bubble: Lilita One, one English line.
- Year tag top left: bold lining "1957".

## 10. Map
- Region: about 94°E–106°E, 0°–8°N (the peninsula, Singapore, Sumatra's east coast). Natural Earth 1:10m coastlines and admin-0; China is not in frame.
- Borders drawn: only the Malaya–Thailand line (the film is about Malaya's extent). Singapore is printed separately: it stayed a British colony in 1957 and keeps a small British patch. That is correct, and the narration doesn't claim otherwise.
- Malaya's 1957 extent = the modern Peninsular Malaysia shape.

## 11. As built (final)
- **Images:** the user made the sheets; the two ChatGPT sheets were used (matching style, every part separate, brass pins at the joints).
- **Narration:** v1 at 0.10 s: "Nineteen fifty-seven. Britain's patch on Malaya won't lie flat any more." (4.43 s). v2 at 5.15 s: "No war. Just talks. And Britain lets go." (2.95 s).
- **Peel geometry:** the corner that won't lie flat is southern Johor, beyond a line across the state, and it peels back to the north-west. From 0 to 3.3 s it flaps on its own, showing its cream back. Malaya grips it at 3.3 s and walks it back with three tugs (3.8 / 4.8 / 5.8 s), each yank springing back a little.
- **Beats:** hops 1.8 / 2.3 / 2.8 s · grip 3.3 · Britain strains from 3.8 · sighs 6.3 ("just talks") · lifts his hand 7.05 ("lets go") · peel 7.3: the whole patch comes up and is flung out of frame, top left (one in-between frame with ink arcs), and the helmet jumps · date slip "31 August 1957" 7.45 · MERDEKA! 7.55 · pin on Kuala Lumpur 7.75 · pull back to the comic page 8.9–9.9 (the panel on the page, the next row of panels empty below it).
- **Ending:** changed from the narrow date panel to the date slip under the year tag, plus a single pull back to the page. At 9:16 and 10 s there was no room for a second panel's reading time.
- **Score:** 120 bpm on a grid from 0.3 s. Britain's tuba and trombone oom-pah thins under the tugs. Hand drums (darbuka doom/tak, standing in for gendang and rebana) answer each tug. A thin violin tremolo plays from 6.3 s. The peel brings a big gong, bass drum and a marimba/glockenspiel run. At the pin the music stops dead with only a small gong ringing, then a soft Bb major chord plays under the pull back.
- **Checks:** speech check 2/2 OK; readcheck OK (tightest: "kuala lumpur" 2.48 s against 2.23 s); −14.0 LUFS; no black frames.
- **Not the demo again:** this film differs from the demo in its structure (one tall panel, one action, against six panels and four changes of hand), its opening (the corner already curling, Britain already pressing, against a puppet hopping in with a patch), its signature move (a peel walked back by tugs, against a cut and a tear), its camera path (one panel with small pushes, against page moves between panels) and its score shape (Britain's brass giving way to Malay hand drums and gong). It shares the pin ending and the pull back to the page (2 of 6).
